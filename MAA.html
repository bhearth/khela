I

The Core Insight — Consciousness Is Not a Thing We Have

Stop asking what consciousness is. Start asking what it does. The move from noun to signature.

The word consciousness — and its Sanskrit cousin chetna, or cit — has been carried for centuries without ever being defined. Philosophy has called this the hard problem. Contemplative traditions have said neti neti, not this, not this. Cognitive science built a whole field around the fact that the word will not hold still.

The insight here is not that the word is vague — many have noticed that. The insight is a move: replace the noun with a measurable verb-phrase. Not consciousness, but the clarity with which one expresses. Not what it is, but what it does.

This is a legitimate operational turn. Behaviourism made the same move with mind. Cognitive science made it with intelligence, replacing the construct with the measurable g. Psychometrics makes it with depression, which no one observes directly — they observe a score on the PHQ-9. The score is not depression. It is depression's operational signature.

You know this already, in your own life. You cannot point to your consciousness. But you can notice the difference between a moment when your response is clear — and a moment when it is muddy, reactive, caught. That difference is what this framework measures.

Eclarity = the operational signature of consciousness Not the thing. The face it shows.

We do not claim to measure consciousness. We claim to measure expressive clarity — the observable signature through which consciousness shows itself. Whether that signature is consciousness itself is a metaphysical question we leave open.

Honest Boundary

A thermometer does not measure "heat" in the metaphysical sense. It measures expansion — a proxy that tracks heat reliably. Eclarity is the same kind of object: a behavioural proxy. It earns its place by predicting outcomes, not by claiming to be the thing itself.

II

The Corrected Model — Two Indices, Not One

The original E = MA² mixed two different things under one letter. Split them, and the whole framework becomes coherent and bounded.

The Problem in the Original Formula

In E = MA², the letter A was used for both awareness and addictive attachments which in itself has addictions. Addictions make us attached. Two different constructs under one symbol collapses the notation. Also, E was unbounded (M = 35,000), so it could not be compared across people or states. Both are fixable — and fixing them produces a stronger framework, not a weaker one.

This is the closest legitimate descendant of your original E. Attachment adds energy here — obsessive, driven, compulsive expression has high volume. It is not "good" or "bad." It is throughput. Bounded by dividing by the maximum possible thought count in the reference population.

Index 2 — Expressive Clarity how clean the flow is

Eclarity = x × (ai × ao) × (1 − a′) Bounded 0 → 1 · High attachment dampens clarity

This is the state-of-mind measure you were looking for. It is the operational signature of consciousness — the clarity with which awareness flows into expression, undimmed by attachment.

Why the product, not the sum

A product captures a real mechanism: if any factor is zero, the output is zero. No awareness → no clear expression. This is not arbitrary. It has precedent: Fishbein's expectancy-value model and Fisher's behaviour model both use multiplicative composites for exactly this reason — because the factors are conditions, not additives.

V

Moha & Maya — Operationalizing Illusion

The tradition says illusion obscures reality. If we take measurement seriously, we must ask: obscure by how much? Both Moha and Maya can be given honest, bounded, testable operational forms.

Moha — delusion, self-misjudgment, the fog around one's own state. Maya — illusion, structural distortion, the world showing itself wrongly. The tradition treats both as obstacles. The framework does not need to dissolve them to measure them. It only needs to ask what their observable signature would be.

Moha · Delusion

Self-Misjudgment Gap

Moha is the gap between what a person believes their clarity is, and what they can demonstrate it is on a calibration task. This is measurable — it is the same structure as the Dunning-Kruger effect, metacognitive calibration, and confidence-accuracy research.

Dmoha = | perceived clarity − demonstrated accuracy |
Range: 0 (well-calibrated) → 1 (fully deluded)

Maya · Illusion

Structural Distortion

Maya is systematic, repeatable bias that persists across contexts. Not random error — patterned distortion. This is measurable through implicit association tests, framing susceptibility, confirmation bias rates, and perceptual illusions that survive correction.

Dmaya = mean bias magnitude across tasks
Range: 0 (no systematic distortion) → 1 (fully distorted)

Both constructs connect to mature research programmes. Metacognitive calibration is a well-developed field (Fleming, Lau, Dunning). Bias measurement is even older (Kahneman & Tversky, Greenwald's IAT). The framework is not inventing these — it is placing them inside a bounded expression model where they do work.

Moha and Maya in the tradition are not merely cognitive errors. They are ontological conditions — the world as such appears under their sway. The operationalization here does not claim to capture that depth. It captures only the shadow they cast on measurable behaviour. The deeper question — whether the world itself is Maya — remains outside the model.

The adjusted clarity index

Eclarity, adj = Eclarity × (1 − Dmoha/2) × (1 − Dmaya/2) Clarity, discounted by self-misjudgment and structural distortion

This is the most honest version of the model: a person's clarity, corrected by how much they are likely to be wrong about their own clarity and about the world. A person can feel clear (high self-reported Eclarity) and yet be deluded (high Dmoha) — the adjusted index catches that. This is where the framework becomes testable against reality, not just descriptive.

What this does not claim

The adjusted index does not capture the full metaphysical weight of Moha and Maya. It captures their measurable behavioural signature. It also does not assume high Dmoha is always bad — a certain amount of confidence is necessary to act at all. The model reports the gap; it does not moralize it.

The Path to Validation — Four Phases

A conceptual framework becomes a scientific instrument through a known sequence. Each phase is achievable.

Phase 1 · Conceptual Paper

Publish the framework

Define constructs, present the multiplicative model, cite Fisher and Fishbein, show the circumplex, state limitations. Timeline: 3–6 months.

Phase 2 · Instrument Development

Build the measure

Item pools → expert review → pilot (n ≈ 300) → exploratory factor analysis → confirmatory factor analysis → reliability (α, ω) → test-retest. Confirm the 4-factor structure.

Phase 3 · Model Comparison

The decisive test

Does the multiplicative composite outperform an additive one (sum of z-scores)? Fit both with structural equation modelling. If multiplication wins on AIC/BIC, that is a publishable empirical result — and the single most important test of the whole model.

Phase 4 · Criterion Validity

Does it predict what it should?

Does Eclarity predict lower reactivity, better well-being, lower experiential avoidance — and not correlate with social desirability? If yes, the construct earns its place.

On random input

Randomly sampling ai, ao, a′, x uniformly on [0,1] and computing the index 100,000 times gives the null distribution. This is not measurement — it is calibration. It answers: "a score of 0.72 is above 99.9% of what chance alone would produce." That is a real statistical step, and it belongs in the paper as a permutation test.
