The biodiversity spectrum · the grace of gross profit

Sun gives. Grass receives. Ocean carries. River returns. Species without a mind catch the whole of it.

The second master picture. The unknown pours down as sunlight. The leaf has no ego to filter it, so it takes the full spectrum. The ocean holds it, the river carries it back, the soil keeps it. Fish, bees, worms, elephants, mangroves — none has a mann, and each catches all of it. The human, the only one with a mind, sees a sliver.

The giver

The sun does not ask

It gives light without condition — to the leaf, the sea, the skin of a child, the stone. No negotiation, no rate, no debt. This is the original gross profit: the sun is paid back only in grass.

The receiver without mind

The grass catches the whole spectrum

A blade of grass has no past regret, no future worry, no pride, no shame. Because there is nothing to filter, the entire spectrum lands. This is why the leaf is the purest listener on Earth.

The carrier

Ocean and river

The ocean holds what the sun gave and the rain returned. The river moves it up, over, back into the field. Neither has a mind. The loop closes because nothing resists.

The givers without name

Species, all of them

Bees carry pollen. Worms turn soil. Fungi link roots. Fish cycle nutrients. Elephants spread seeds. Vultures clean. Plankton breathe. None write a ledger. All run the credit of the planet.

The grace of gross profit

Gross Profit is not only what a company earned. It is what a place gained — soil, water, species, children, tools, skills, all together. The first Gross Profit on Earth was a leaf: it took sun, air, water and a mineral and gave back sugar and breath.

Layer 2 · Bina Mann · बिना मन

Biodiversity — grass, tulsi, herbs, trees, worms, bees, rivers — has no mann, no ego, no goal, no desire, no past regret, no future worry. And yet it is perfectly active. Layer 3 · Earth Hora · पृथ्वी होरा

One hour of silence, at the same time, every day

The sages left one remedy. Not a philosophy. Not a system. A single hour. Not meditation performed for a result. Silence kept for its own sake. The parashar vigyan. The science that heals the mind so that the four aims can fulfill themselves.

What the hour does

Three changes, in the order they arrive

Week one to four

The nervous system settles

The vagus nerve recovers its tone. The gut-brain axis quiets. This is healing. It is not a feeling. It is a mechanical state.

Week four to twelve

Sleep becomes yoga-nidra

The hour colors the other twenty-three. Sleep stops being a replay of yesterday's insults and tomorrow's fears.

Month three onward

The transmission returns

The same cosmic current that reaches the tulsi, the cow, the river begins to reach the human again. The hour restores the antenna.

Why one hour and not five minutes

The mind has a long tail. Twenty minutes of quiet is followed by forty minutes of catch-up. One hour is the threshold where the noise finishes its backlog and the being sits in actual quiet for a sustained stretch.

E/arth · Bina Mann · Akarshan — The Biodiversity Spectrum · Krishna Bharat

An e/arth inquiry

Why no one knows what to do

Nobody was handed the manual. Not your parents. Not your teachers. Not the elders. Not the community. Not the corporations. Not the governments. Not the journalists. Not the films. Not the feeds. And yet every person is expected to know how to keep a body healthy, a mind steady, an energy alive — and how to raise their own consciousness.

Access — often missing Understanding — often missing Willingness — a deeper mystery Action — rarely follows

The honest admission

It is not that people refuse to learn.

It is that the conditions for learning were never built. When a person has been given no fair chance to know — no access, no explanation in their language, no time, no safety, no one to ask without shame — the absence of clarity is not a personal failure. It is a structural one.

A person cannot act on knowledge they never had a fair opportunity to receive. — the deeper principle

And after enough failed attempts to understand, something worse happens than ignorance: wanting fades. The person who "does not want to learn" is often a person who tried, was humiliated, was misled, and stopped. That is not laziness. That is an injury.

But there is a further step that almost no one talks about. Even when access exists, even when understanding is possible, even when the solution is simple and helps everyone — the action still does not happen. A village that could build a biogas plant from its own cattle dung, feed its soil, light its stoves, and stop cutting trees — does not build it. Not because it is hard. Because no one has ever shown them it is possible, no one around them has done it, and no story, film, news report, or elder has ever said: this is what people like us do now.

Diagram 01

The chain of failure — four gates, and almost no one gets through all four

Knowledge does not fail at one point. It fails at four, in sequence. And each failure looks like something else — laziness, stubbornness, ignorance — when it is really a broken link in a chain that no one built.

Gate 01

Access

Is the information, the news, the opportunity, the training even available? In many places — no. No internet, no library, no mentor, no demonstration, no one who has ever done it.

Without access — the question never even arises.
Gate 02

Comprehension

If the information exists, is it in a language, format, and context the person can actually understand? Or is it buried in jargon, English, PDFs, and assumptions?

Without comprehension — the information is noise.
Gate 03

Willingness

Even when understood, does the person want to act? Habit, exhaustion, distrust, fear of looking foolish, no visible example — all push toward "we have always done it this way."

Without willingness — knowledge sits unused.
Gate 04

Action

Even when willing, can they act? Do they have time, money, tools, permission, safety, and a way to try without ruinous risk?

Without action — nothing changes. The next generation inherits the gap.

Most discussions stop at gate one or two. But the deepest problem is gate three: willingness. It is not stupidity or stubbornness. It is the absence of a living example — someone like them, in a place like theirs, who has already done it.

Diagram 02

Where the flow narrows

Picture knowledge as a river trying to reach a field. Four gates stand in the way. At each gate, most of the water is lost — and no one downstream can tell whether the drought was caused by gate one, two, three, or four.

At each gate, the flow narrows. By the end, almost nothing reaches the field — and everyone downstream wonders why the harvest failed.

Diagram 03

Three things that are simple, help everyone, and still are not done

These are not complex problems. They are not expensive. They do not require new science. They fail at one or more of the four gates — and because no one has ever pointed at them clearly, they keep failing in silence.

Gate 03 · Willingness

Biogas in a small village

Cattle dung becomes cooking gas and fertiliser. It cuts smoke, saves trees, saves money, and improves soil. The technology is decades old. The village has the dung. The need is obvious.

It is not built — because no one nearby has done it, no film has shown it, no elder has demonstrated it, and the first person to try would be alone.

Gate 01 & 02 · Access + Comprehension

No child gets good nourishment

Not because food does not exist. Because the knowledge of what a child actually needs — protein, iron, diversity, not just calories — never reaches the household in a usable form.

The information exists in reports, in English, in PDFs. It does not exist in the kitchen, in the language of the mother, in the rhythm of the day.

Gate 01 · Access

No play, no playgrounds

Children need movement, risk, imagination, and time outdoors — not as a luxury, but as a foundation of body, mind, and social learning. Yet in countless places there is no safe ground, no equipment, no time.

The need is known. The solution is a patch of earth, a swing, a ball, and permission. It still is not built.

These are not failures of intelligence. They are failures of a system that never showed anyone what is possible — and then blamed them for not doing it.

The deeper vocabulary

Five capacities that almost no system teaches

These are not decorations. They are the actual skills a human needs to navigate a world that no generation has faced before. They are rarely taught in schools, rarely modelled by elders, and rarely shown in media.

Prajñā · प्रज्ञा

The ability to understand wisely.

Deeper than collecting information. Seeing relationships, recognising consequences, questioning assumptions, and knowing the limits of one's knowledge.

Dharma · धर्म

The responsibility to act appropriately.

Ethical conduct, duty, right relationship, the order that sustains life. It requires context, fairness, compassion, and attention to consequences.

Perfection · पूर्णता

The continual improvement of what matters.

Not never making mistakes. Becoming more accurate, capable, compassionate, and responsible — while recognising that every human has limits.

Viveka · विवेक

The ability to discriminate and choose.

What is useful versus harmful? What is urgent versus important? What do I know, and what am I assuming?

Karunā · करुणा

The willingness to understand another's difficulty.

A person who struggles may need accessible information, more time, safety, practical support, or a different way of learning — not blame.

These are related concepts, not identical.
Their precise meanings vary across Indian philosophical and religious traditions.

The human awareness cycle

Five steps that almost no one is taught to complete

This is the cycle that turns a person from a passive receiver into an active participant in their own life. It is rarely taught. It is almost never practised.

1 · Ask

What is happening? What do I need to know? The willingness to admit that you do not know is the beginning of all learning.

2 · Discover

Find reliable information from people, records, experience and trusted sources. Not the loudest — the most reliable.

3 · Understand

Check what is true, what is uncertain, and what affects me and others. Understanding is not agreement — it is clarity.

4 · Decide

Choose the next useful action based on understanding, not fear or rumours. Small, safe, reversible steps are enough.

5 · Share & update

Communicate what was learned, help others, and keep learning. Knowledge that is not shared dies with the person who found it.

This cycle is not a straight line.
It is a loop that turns again
with every new situation.

Practical wisdom

What should a human do when the necessary knowledge is missing?

These are not slogans. They are the actual moves available to a person who finds themselves at the edge of what they know.

  1. Recognise the gap. "I don't know yet" is a strength, not a weakness. Naming the gap is the first act of intelligence.
  2. Ask someone who knows. Seek explanations without shame. The shame of not knowing is the thing that keeps people ignorant.
  3. Check multiple reliable sources. Distinguish evidence from opinion, advertising and misinformation. One source is never enough.
  4. Find the missing update. Is the information unavailable, too complicated, inaccessible, outdated, or simply not communicated? The answer changes the fix.
  5. Take one safe, useful step. Do not wait for perfect knowledge when a small, reversible action can help. Action teaches what information cannot.
  6. Create a feedback loop. If important information is repeatedly missing, improve the system that delivers it. Do not just solve the immediate problem — fix the pipe.

Diagram 07

Six conditions — and why shouting louder never works

A person may struggle not because they lack intelligence, but because one or more of these conditions is missing. When a step is missing, repeating the same instruction louder does not help. The system has to change.

If information is unavailable, too complicated, inaccessible, outdated, or simply never communicated — the fix is not more effort from the learner. The fix is at the level of the system that failed to deliver it.

Diagram 08

The bridge nobody built

The young need guidance and inspiration. The old are supposed to give it. But the old were never taught either — and are now carrying their own survival. Neither side can be pressured into a bridge that was never constructed.

We cannot pressure the old

They are not withholding wisdom out of spite. Many are exhausted, unwell, financially frightened, and were themselves never given a single hour of instruction on the mind, the body or consciousness. Demanding guidance from someone who never received it just adds shame to an already heavy load.

We cannot abandon the young

The next generation is being asked to solve problems no generation has faced — with tools that were designed to capture them, not to teach them. They need something other than blame, and something other than a lecture.

Diagram 09

Compassion and accountability are not opposites

Understanding why a person did not know is not the same as saying that nothing can be corrected. Both are needed — and both can be held at once.

Being inexperienced does not make every action harmless, and every claim equally valid. But neither does harm-making become acceptable simply because someone was ignorant. The mature position is: investigate, correct, protect — and still refuse to humiliate the person who was never given the chance.

Diagram 10

Nobody is fully innocent here — and nobody is fully to blame. Each system has a specific blindness, and a specific thing it could actually do.

CouldMake room for questions no one in the family was ever allowed to ask.

“Teach facts, rarely judgement, health or how to live.”

CouldTeach discernment, not just information. Teach the body and the mind as subjects.

“Carries tradition and gossip in the same breath.”

CouldVerify before passing it on. Share what actually worked, not only what was said.

“Rewards attention, not understanding.”

CouldExplain causes, uncertainty and consequences — not only events.

“Optimise output; treat body and mind as inputs.”

CouldCount health, time and dignity as real costs, not externalities.

“Publishes information, rarely ensures it lands.”

CouldReach people in the language and place they actually live.

Technology platforms

“Amplify what holds attention, not what helps.”

CouldSurface what is timely, true and actionable — and say what is unknown.

The practical ethic

What should we do — and what should we avoid?

These are not abstract ideals. They are the daily choices that determine whether a person, a family, or a community moves toward clarity or deeper confusion.

Do

  • Make knowledge accessible in different languages and formats.
  • Teach through examples, practice, questions, and experience.
  • Admit uncertainty and correct mistakes.
  • Consider the effects of decisions on other people, animals, and ecosystems.
  • Give people the time, resources, and dignity needed to learn.

Avoid

  • Blaming people for knowledge they never had a fair chance to acquire.
  • Confusing confidence, popularity, or authority with truth.
  • Demanding identical learning speeds from everyone.
  • Acting on rumours when verification is possible.
  • Treating perfection as an excuse for shame, exclusion, or endless delay.

Diagram 12

The role of journalism, media, entertainment, and social media

Humanity is learning how to live while living. There is no complete user manual for being human. Each communication system has a distinct responsibility — and each is currently failing in its own way.

Journalism

Helping humanity understand reality.

Reveal what is happening, investigate why, explain who is affected, examine evidence, uncover knowledge people need. Its duty is not merely to report events, but to make reality more understandable.

News media

Helping people stay informed.

Alert people to developments, risks, opportunities, public decisions, scientific discoveries. The challenge is to distinguish what is urgent from what is important, and what is confirmed from what remains unknown.

Entertainment

Helping people experience possibilities.

Stories, films, music, comedy, and drama allow people to explore emotions, relationships, choices, mistakes, courage, and consequences. It can also simply provide joy, rest, and connection.

Social media

Helping experience travel between people.

Connect people facing similar challenges, spread practical knowledge, enable collective problem-solving. But speed is not the same as truth, popularity is not the same as wisdom.

No child gets good nutrition. No village builds its biogas. No community builds a playground. And no film, no news report, no feed, no story has ever shown them it is possible. That is not a failure of the people. It is a failure of the systems that were supposed to show them.

None can replace the others.

What would have to be built

Seven obligations of a human knowledge system

A society cannot anticipate every future circumstance, so it cannot write one manual with all the answers. What it can build is a system that keeps learning.

  1. Collect experiences without humiliating the people who struggled through them.
  2. Verify facts, and clearly separate them from interpretation and opinion.
  3. Explain difficult subjects in languages, formats and places people actually have access to.
  4. Make practical knowledge available at the moment it is needed, not after.
  5. Share lessons from successes, failures and unintended consequences alike.
  6. Measure success by improved understanding and human & ecological wellbeing — not by views, revenue or urgency.
  7. Allow people to question, correct and improve the knowledge system itself.

This connects directly to the idea of an e/arth journalism: a publication would not merely tell people what happened. It would help them understand what happened, what it means, what choices are available, and what humanity can learn.

The deepest principle

Everyone is living their life for the first time

This is the sentence that dissolves blame. No parent has done this before. No elder has faced these exact conditions. No community has navigated this particular collision of tradition, technology, pollution, and speed. No journalist has reported on a world that changes faster than the reporting cycle. No government has governed a species this large, this connected, this confused.

No one has the ability to know what to do — because no one has ever been here before. — and yet everyone is expected to act as if they have

This is not an excuse. It is a diagnosis. If no one has ever faced this, then the solution cannot be to find the one person who already knows. The solution must be to build systems that help everyone learn together — faster, more honestly, and with less shame.

The biogas is not built because no one has seen it built. The child is not fed because no one has shown the mother what to cook. The playground is not made because no one has said: this is what a community does for its children. The elder does not guide because the elder was never guided.

None of this is anyone's fault. And all of it is everyone's responsibility.

The question that remains

If a human does not know what they need to know — who or what should help them discover it?

Not by delivering more information. Not by blaming them for the gap. Not by waiting for the perfect elder or the perfect parent or the perfect government that will never arrive.

But by building systems that help each person discover the right knowledge, at the right time, for the right reason — with dignity, in their language, and with the honesty to say what is still unknown.

No human is born with a complete manual for life. Every generation inherits knowledge, meets new circumstances, makes mistakes, and discovers possibilities. The responsibility of communication is to help humanity learn from that journey — without humiliation, without hiding truth, and without confusing attention with understanding. The guiding principle

And the ultimate test of any communication system becomes this: after receiving the information, is a person better equipped to understand their situation, make an informed choice, and contribute to the wellbeing of others?

Layer 13 · The Rainbow Body · इन्द्रधनुष

We have little bodies, and still we do not look up.

We are the only species with language, hands that transform matter, and a mind that can imagine what has never been. We have looked at the stars and named them. We have measured the universe. And yet most of us spend our lives fighting over words, defending small egos, and never once asking the question that all existence asks: why is there something rather than nothing?

Speak — but do we understand? Correct — without humiliating Elevate — the whole planet Wonder — the source

The spectrum we can see

The rainbow is the interface

The colors we can see — red through violet — are a tiny sliver of what exists. Below red: infrared, radio, microwaves, the cosmic background. Above violet: ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. And behind it all: the dark. The dark is not empty. It is the source.

White is not a color — it is the sum

We see white when all the colors of the visible spectrum arrive together. We see black when none arrive. But the light itself is invisible. We only see what it touches. We see the effect of light, never light itself.

Every being is a rainbow

Every species carries a small band of the universe's capacity to perceive. The bee sees ultraviolet we cannot. The snake sees infrared. The bat navigates by sound. The tree reads the direction of the sun. We see our band — and mistake it for the whole.

The biodiversity spectrum — the full spread of life on Earth — is itself a rainbow. Every species is one band. Every band is needed for the whole. The white at the center is the whole, seen all at once. The dark around is the source, still invisible.

The cosmic multiplicity

Every galaxy is one of a kind

Two trillion galaxies. Each with a hundred billion suns. No two alike. Every planetary system is unique. Every species on every planet, if there are others, unique. The cosmos is not a factory — it is a fractal of one-of-a-kind.

We have not found life elsewhere. Perhaps it is rare. Perhaps it is everywhere and we do not know how to look. What we do know: this small blue planet is not a copy. It is not a prototype. It is one expression of a universe that has never repeated itself. The same is true of you.

Two forms of learning

Nature is a university without walls. AI is a university without a mind.

The biological classroom

Primates

Chimpanzees and other primates learn through observation, imitation, tool use, and social transmission. Jane Goodall's observations helped establish that traditions pass between generations — without a single word.

Birds

Many birds learn calls, recognise individuals, solve problems, and adapt behaviour. Some associate human sounds with objects. This is not mastery of human language — it is a different kind of intelligence.

Cattle & domestic animals

Animals learn routines, signals, associations. Good training depends on understanding their abilities, needs, and welfare — not on repeating commands louder.

The living world

Species survive through evolved adaptations and individual learning. They do not need to understand the science of their own survival. They simply live it.

The machine classroom

SystemHow it learns
AnimalsObservation, association, practice, social learning, feedback
HumansAll of the above, plus language, abstraction, reflection, and cumulative teaching
AI & MLTraining data, statistical patterns, objectives, feedback, evaluation

AI can process at remarkable speed. But producing an answer quickly does not guarantee that it is true, wise, or appropriate. The human task is not to compete with the machine — it is to remain the one who checks, corrects, and cares.

The shared lesson is not that all minds work alike. It is that learning environments, experience, feedback, and opportunities to practise matter — for chimps, for children, and for models.

The extra capacity — and its cost

What only humans can do. And what it costs us.

The capacity

  • Speak — with grammar, metaphor, and abstraction.
  • Walk upright — hands free to hold, shape, transform.
  • Build tools that build other tools.
  • Teach what we learned to someone who was not there.
  • Imagine what has never been, and then make it.

The cost

  • The mind loses most of its energy in unnecessary thought.
  • We fight about words and call it truth.
  • We defend egos and call it principle.
  • We forget to look up and call it being practical.
  • The grass does not do this. The bird does not do this. They live in the same cosmos and are not confused by it.

The extra capacity is real. The cost is real too. The question is not whether we can speak — we can. The question is whether we can use speech to reduce suffering or to create it. That choice is the actual evolution.

The medicines

The medicines of the tongue

These are not magic words. Their value is not in a special frequency. Their value is in the meaning, sincerity, timing, and actions that accompany them. Said well, at the right moment, they are medicine. Said carelessly, they are noise.

धन्यवाद

Thank you

Recognition. Acknowledge effort, time, kindness, contribution. Specific appreciation tells someone exactly what was valuable.

कृपया

Please

Respectful request. Invite cooperation rather than assume entitlement. Honour the other's right to decline.

क्षमा

I'm sorry

Accountability. Recognise the effect of an action, listen to the person affected, make a genuine effort to repair.

क्षमा करता हूँ

I forgive

Releasing resentment when possible. It does not deny harm, abandon boundaries, or reconcile with an unsafe person.

मैं साथ हूँ

I care

Compassion. Ask what support is needed, listen without rushing to judge, offer help that respects the person's wishes.

शाबाश

That's great!

Encouragement. Recognise progress, creativity, courage, effort. Most useful when sincere and grounded in what actually happened.

कैसे किया?

How did you do that?

Curiosity. Invite someone to explain their process. Instead of merely admiring the outcome, discover the knowledge behind it.

अद्भुत

Awesome

Wonder. Not a filler word — an actual pause. The willingness to be stopped by something greater than you.

कृपा

Grace

Acknowledging what we did not earn — the light, the breath, the ground, the person who forgave us. The root of every gratitude.

The art, not the rule

What to say — and when, how, why, where, to whom

The same sentence can help in one situation and harm in another. Human communication therefore requires more than memorising polite expressions. It is an art — learned through observation, error, correction, and love.

WHO?

Understand the person, their role, experience, preferences, and boundaries.

WHAT?

Choose words relevant to the actual situation, not to the generic script.

WHEN?

Consider whether the person is ready to listen, or needs immediate help, time, or privacy.

HOW?

Adapt your language, tone, pace, detail, and communication method.

WHY?

Know whether your purpose is to inform, ask, appreciate, correct, comfort, or protect.

WHERE?

Choose a suitable setting: private, public, written, spoken, accessible, quiet.

If someone is struggling to understand a task, ordering them may not help. Demonstrate, ask what is unclear, offer a simpler explanation. If someone has made a mistake, immediate public criticism may create shame rather than learning. A respectful explanation, clear boundaries, and an opportunity to correct may work better.

The goal is not to speak perfectly in every moment. It is to become more capable of recognising what the moment requires. This cannot be taught as fixed rules. It is an art.

The cycle of evolution

Human evolution through continuous correction

The more we focus on correcting and improving — individually and collectively — the more the entire planet becomes a creative powerhouse. This is not a policy. It is a practice.

The settled nervous system

Becoming supernatural — what Dispenza proposes

Joe Dispenza proposes that the human nervous system is not fixed. Through meditation, breathwork, and mental rehearsal, a person can shift the state of the body and the brain — from survival to creation, from habit to choice. He calls this becoming supernatural. What he describes is not magic. It is the natural capacity of a settled nervous system.

The body believes the mind

The body lives in the emotion of its past. When we rehearse the future in the present moment, the body begins to believe it has already happened — and rehearses a new self.

Breaking the habit of being yourself

Most people live in a loop of the same thoughts, the same emotions, the same identity. The observer who watches the loop can step outside it.

The coherent heart

Heart-brain coherence — measured as a smooth rhythm between the two — is associated with slower breathing, calm attention, and emotional balance. Meditation is one way to approach it.

His work is presented here as a proposal, not as established science. What matters is the direction: a settled nervous system, a coherent heart, a quiet mind — the same direction every tradition has pointed to. Earth Hora is a version of this. Bina Mann is the diagnosis. Akarshan is the pull.

The oldest question

Nāsadīya Sūkta · नासदीय सूक्त

Rig Veda, Mandala 10, Hymn 129. Five thousand years old. It is not an answer. It is a question — the most honest question in the human tradition. It does not know. It says so. And in saying so, it becomes the highest form of curiosity.

नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीं नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत्।
nāsad āsīn no sad āsīt tadānīṁ nāsīd rajo no vyomā paro yat |
Then there was neither being nor non-being; there was no realm of air, no sky beyond it.
न मृत्युरासीदमृतं न तर्हि न रात्र्या अह्न आसीत्प्रकेतः।
na mṛtyur āsīd amṛtaṁ na tarhi na rātryā ahna āsīt praketaḥ |
Death was not then, nor was there anything immortal: no sign was there, the day's and night's divider.
को अद्धा वेद क इह प्र वोचत्कुत आजाता कुत इयं विसृष्टिः।
ko addhā veda ka iha pra vocat kuta ājātā kuta iyaṁ visṛṣṭiḥ |
Who really knows? Who can here proclaim it? Whence, whence this creation sprang?
इयं विसृष्टिर्यत आबभूव यदि वा दधे यदि वा न।
iyaṁ visṛṣṭir yata ābabhūva yadi vā dadhe yadi vā na |
Whence this creation has arisen — perhaps it formed itself, or perhaps it did not — the One who looks down on it, in the highest heaven, only He knows — or perhaps even He does not know.

The sages did not claim to know. They claimed the question. That is the highest curiosity — not I know, but I do not know, and I want to look.

The principle

Learn from every species. Learn from every experience. Speak according to understanding. Correct without humiliation. Appreciate what helps life flourish.

That is a practical vision of human evolution: not perfect people, but a continually learning civilisation. Not a species that has arrived, but a species that is still looking up.
