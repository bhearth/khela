Earth population and quality vs quantity

Quantity is counted everywhere. Quality, how well each being lives, is counted in far fewer places. A child journalist's first job is to tell the two apart.

8.3 billion
People alive in 2026, about 69 million more each year, heading for around 10 billion by 2060. (UN)
148 million
Children under 5 stunted in 2022, plus 45 million wasted. (UNICEF, WHO, World Bank)
49,505
Species threatened out of 175,909 assessed, in 2026. Most species have not been assessed. (IUCN)
73%
Average fall in monitored wildlife populations, 1970 to 2022. About half of them are stable or rising. (WWF, ZSL)

The first number keeps growing. The other three are what quality looks like when nobody is watching it.

Types of reality

Six ways of knowing what is real

A journalist asks how she knows what she knows. Tap each kind of reality, and the question it asks of you.

Life being born, mutating and ending

Earth's kids, species, fruits, vegetables and herbs

All of it is real at once. New life arrives, forms change, and some forms end.

Being born

Every generation is a little different. Each child carries dozens of new changes in its DNA, and so does every plant, insect and fungus. Most are harmless, a few help or harm. This quiet variation is how life explores.

Mutating, naturally

Nectarines arose from peaches by a natural mutation. Cabbage, kale, broccoli and cauliflower all descend from one wild plant, shaped by farmers who kept the interesting mutants. Herbs vary plant to plant in scent and leaf, so farmers save seed from the best.

Ending

Extinction is natural, but today's pace is not. Habitat loss, overuse, climate change, pollution and invasive species are the main pressures. The wild cousins of our crops, and old local varieties, are among those at risk.

Of more than 6,000 plant species cultivated for food, only nine account for about two thirds of crop production. (FAO, 2019)

Covered by bio-engineering

Three ways people steer life

Oldest

Selection and breeding

Keep the seed of the plant you love. Landraces and heirloom varieties are thousands of years of such choices. Seed banks and seed-saving farmers keep the spectrum alive.

Faster

Mutation breeding

Seeds or cuttings are exposed to radiation or chemicals to speed up mutation, and the useful results are kept. More than 3,000 crop varieties released worldwide came this way.

Direct

Genetic engineering and gene editing

Genes are moved or edited on purpose: insect-resistant cotton, vitamin-enriched rice, disease-resistant crops. Powerful, and it raises questions of safety, ownership and diversity.

The quality test for any bio-engineering

Does it raise quality, not only quantity? Does it keep diversity alive? Who gets the benefit, and who cares for it afterwards? Bio-engineering is a tool. The purpose decides whether it feeds a few or nourishes all.

The spark of Earth-oh-city

Organic electricity: life force that moves without a brain

Plants, fungi, cows and elephants carry programming older than any neuron, and most of it is about giving. Children arrive with the newest neurons. Between the two lies the mystery this page follows: how to reach the source of life force, and how to carry it on to others.

Where a neuron comes from

The same stem cells grow thick or thin, depending on what reaches them

Tradition says one cosmic energy moves through the body's sheaths. Science says nutrition, clean air and water, sleep, safety and stress shape how a child's neurons grow. Turn the four dials. Each is a veil that can thin what arrives.

100% reaches the neuron

An illustration of an idea, not a measurement. Dots are energy and nourishment travelling inward; each veil can stop some of them.

Established biology: most neurons are made before birth. Adult neurogenesis continues in places such as the hippocampus, though how much occurs in adult humans is still debated.

The next five sheaths

From inner joy to lifting the whole treasure house

Kosha means both sheath and treasure house. Tradition's five sheaths end at ānandamaya, inner joy. These five begin there and carry the treasure outward, one ring at a time. Tap a ring, and switch between what each sheath means and what science says.

Below ānandamaya sits the traditional foundation: annamaya (food), prāṇamaya (breath), manomaya (impressions) and vijñānamaya (understanding). Ānandamaya comes from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad. The four after it are this page's extension.

A thread from the classics

Muditā-vijñāna leans on Patanjali's Yoga Sutra 1.33, which names friendliness (maitrī), compassion (karuṇā), gladness (muditā) and equanimity (upekṣā) as the attitudes that settle the mind. Settled, the mind can understand others clearly, and rejoice with them.

Utthāna and demand

Demand lifts the treasure house

The thesis: when beings ask, the source answers with preraṇā, inspiration, and preraṇā becomes prāṇa, life force. The asking has to come from all beings. If only a few can voice a need, most of the treasure goes unused and only those few receive. Slide to change who can ask.

Each dot is a being: children, trees, fungi, bees, cows, elephants, worms, birds and others. Lit dots can voice a demand. A thesis shown as a picture, not a measurement. In real ecosystems and societies, who gets heard depends on many things.

Organic programming

Life that runs without mind, brain or neurons

Long before neurons, living things sensed, signalled and organised. Humans are one design among millions, and many of the others are built to give.

Plants

No brain, yet roots, leaves and flowers pass electrical and chemical signals. A wounded leaf can warn the rest of the plant within minutes.

Fungi

Underground threads link tree roots and move water and minerals between them. How much of this amounts to sharing is still debated.

Every living cell

Cells across living tissue, not only neurons, hold electrical voltage. Researchers study how these bioelectric patterns help shape bodies as they grow.

Tap a life form to see what it gives, and what breaks if it goes.

Ecology answers "why so many species?" as roles: each is part of how the others stay alive, humans included. By one estimate (Bar-On, Phillips and Milo, 2018), humans are about 0.01% of Earth's living biomass, while plants are around 80%. Whether life has a purpose beyond roles is a question science leaves open, and tradition answers in its own way.

Every species is unique, and so is its span of life

Something sets how long each creature lives, and how it lives. Genes, repair systems and metabolism explain part of it. Why each species is set to its particular span is still an open question, and this page keeps it open.

Science already links poor diet, sleep loss and chronic stress to low energy, anxiety and weaker social understanding. The full chain, and the idea of prana drawn from too few or too stale sources, is this page's working hypothesis, to be observed and tested in the field.

Early prototype

A way to feel how life forms are doing

An attempt to measure overall satisfaction across life forms, not just humans. Choose a life form, then rate each condition from its point of view, using what you have observed. Rate as many as you can.

- Rate at least one life form to see the shared score.

The shared score is the average across every life form you rated. The lowest point matters more: a living system is only as well as its most struggling member. Ratings stay in this page for this visit only and are not saved or sent anywhere. This is a reflective prototype, not a validated scale.

To become a real measure

  • Define observable signs for each condition per species, such as nesting success in birds, leaf health in trees, soil life for worms.
  • Record the same signs at the same places across seasons.
  • Let many observers, children included, contribute from the field.
  • Compare with existing ecological and animal-welfare indicators before drawing conclusions.
